Coding guide: social support type of forum opening posts

Version 1 draft (2026-11-24), for confirmation at the calibration meeting. It replaces version 0 and is written as signal rules: label a post by the signals it contains, not by what the poster might be feeling beyond the text. The classifier prompts (version 4) use the same definitions and the same decision rules, word for word where possible.

1. What you are coding

Each item is the opening post of a thread in the Asthma UK community on HealthUnlocked, shown as title and body. Record one label per post in up to three steps. Work on your own, do not discuss individual posts with the other coders, and write a note whenever you are unsure.

2. Definitions

Request. Something the poster asks readers for: a direct question, or a request for advice, information or others' experience; an implicit question ("I wonder if...", "not sure whether I should..."); a validation question about a feeling ("Does anyone else feel...?", "Am I overreacting?"); an explicit request for reassurance or encouragement. Conversational closers are not requests: "Thoughts?", "Anyone else?", "Let me know what you think", "Any questions?", "Have you tried X?" (a suggestion).

Feeling statement. The poster states their own emotion in the first person: "I feel / I am / I'm" + an emotion word (worried, scared, anxious, fed up, frustrated, down, overwhelmed, dreading, terrified, desperate), or helplessness ("I can't cope", "at my wit's end", "don't know what else to do", "it's all too much"), or a venting label ("sorry for the rant"). Not feeling statements: bodily states (tired, exhausted, breathless, "breathing is exhausting"); worrying content without a stated emotion (an account of an A&E visit, a relative's illness); apologies, emojis, hopes and pleasantries ("sorry this isn't a question", "fingers crossed", "glad to find this site").

Strong feeling. Any one of: a feeling statement with an intensifier or a strong word ("so / really / very / absolutely" + emotion word; terrified, desperate, fed up, overwhelmed, dreading); helplessness; a venting label; an explicit request for reassurance or encouragement. A single mild emotion word used as a lead-in to a question ("worried", "concerned", "puzzled", "shocked", "a bit nervous", "scared to try X") is not a strong feeling.

Written for others. The post exists to give readers something: information, tips, resources, or experience told so that others can use it; advice or suggestions, including in question form; comfort, empathy or encouragement addressed to readers; announcements, opinion pieces, warnings, campaigns and petitions addressed to the community, by a member or an organisation, even when written with feeling. Not written for others: facts about the poster's own case mentioned in a thanks post, an introduction or an update.

3. Step 1: primary intent (Seeking, Providing, None)

Apply in order:

  1. The post contains a request, and the request is what the post is for: Seeking.
  2. The post is written for others and contains no request beyond a conversational closer: Providing.
  3. Both a request and content for others: remove the question in your mind. If the post still stands as a complete contribution and the sharing is about 80% or more of it, Providing; if the sharing is background leading up to the question, Seeking; still undecided, Seeking.
  4. No request and not written for others (an account of the poster's own events, treatments, appointments or news): with a feeling statement, Seeking; without one, None.
  5. Greetings, introductions, thanks, good news, humour and casual talk with no request: None.

Examples: "Had a flare-up last month, now a cold has gone to my chest. Seeing the asthma nurse tomorrow. Sorry, this wasn't really a question!" None. "My asthma has been terrible and the medications aren't helping. I'm so fed up, I don't know what to do anymore." Seeking. "Our petition has 22,000 signatures. Please sign and spread the word." Providing. "Thank you to my GP for calling me in for my booster. One jab in each arm today." None.

4. Step 2: type of support

4a. Seeking (Informational, Emotional, Both)

An informational question is a question or request whose answer would be facts, advice or others' experience. Every "has anyone else ...?" question whose object is a thing or a situation counts: a drug, device, test, treatment, service, symptom, diagnosis, or "been in a similar situation". A generic "any advice?" counts. A validation question has a feeling or state of mind as its object ("Does anyone else feel exhausted by all this?", "Am I overreacting?").

  1. Is there an informational question?
  2. Is there an emotional component: a validation question or a strong feeling?
  3. Both yes: Both. Informational only: Informational. No informational question: Emotional (including posts with no question at all).

Examples: "I'm worried about my peak flow dropping - should I increase my preventer?" Informational. "Has anyone been on Nucala? ... I'm a bit nervous beginning it all." Informational (mild word). "Anybody else been on this who can give me some encouragement?" Both. "I am so fed up and don't know what else to do. Sorry for the rant, but has anyone been in a similar situation?" Both. "Does anyone else feel like their life revolves around asthma?" Emotional.

4b. Providing (Informational, Emotional, Both)

Practical content is something a reader could learn from or act on: facts, tips, resources, advice, suggestions, an experience told so others can use it, an announcement, warning, campaign or petition. Support addressed to readers is comfort, empathy, encouragement or validation directed at other people and forming real content of the post. Not support: formulaic openers and closers ("Sorry to hear that", "Hope this helps", "Good luck", "Best wishes", "Keep well", "Hope it works for you"); emotion in the poster's own story (their own joy, horror or frustration); a warm tone.

  1. Is there practical content?
  2. Is there support addressed to readers?
  3. Both yes: Both. Practical only: Informational. No practical content: Emotional.

5. Step 3: posts coded None (COM or Ambiguous)

Before confirming None, check once more whether the post contains a request, is written for others, or is an account with a feeling statement; if so, go back to Step 1. Otherwise:

COM (companionship). Greetings and introductions, thanks, updates and good news, humour and casual conversation, accounts of events without a feeling statement.

Ambiguous. A fragment, near-empty, garbled, spam or a bare link, so that no intent can be read.

Final labels: Seeking + Informational = SIS; Seeking + Emotional = SES; Seeking + Both = SIS + SES; Providing + Informational = PIS; Providing + Emotional = PES; Providing + Both = PIS + PES; None + COM = COM; None + Ambiguous = excluded.
