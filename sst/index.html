Coding guide: social support type of forum opening posts

Version 0.1 (draft for the first-author trial, 2026-11-17; decision-order blocks added the same day after 14 trial posts; question-object rule, campaign rule and own-story rule added 2026-11-23 after the 20-post trial). Condensed from the classifier prompts in lurking/prompts/hierarchical_step*.txt; every definition, indicator and decision rule below is taken from those prompts without change, and the examples are theirs. Not yet frozen: wording and examples may change after the 20-post trial and the calibration meeting; category boundaries may not. Sentences in red were added on 2026-11-23 after the trial and are to be confirmed at the calibration meeting.

1. What you are coding

Each row of the sheet is the opening post of a thread in the Asthma UK community on HealthUnlocked, shown as its title and body. Dates, view counts and usernames have been removed. Read the whole post, then record one label per post following the three steps below. Work on your own: do not discuss individual posts with the other coders until the calibration meeting, and do not look anything up. Record your doubts in the notes column rather than leaving a row blank.

Codes record what the poster is trying to do, not how good the post is or how you would reply to it.

2. Step 1: primary intent (Seeking, Providing, None)

Seeking. The poster wants to obtain something from others: information, advice, experiences or emotional comfort. Signals: direct questions; implicit questions ("I wonder if...", "Not sure what to do..."); describing a problem while hoping for input; asking about others' experiences in order to learn; expressing distress while looking for comfort or reassurance.

Providing. The poster wants to give something to others: information, advice, resources or emotional support. Signals: sharing tips, resources or useful information; advice drawn from personal experience; answering others' problems; encouragement or comfort; educational content or announcements to the community. Opinion pieces, warnings, campaigns and petitions addressed to the community, by a member or an organisation, are Providing (Informational) even when written with feeling, as long as nothing is asked for the poster.

None. The post neither seeks nor provides support; it is social interaction. Signals: status updates with no request for advice; greetings, introductions, farewells; thanks for past help; humour and casual conversation; diary-style entries that expect no helpful reply.

The primary purpose test (for posts that mix elements)

Many posts both share and ask. Decide the main reason the person posted:

  1. Remove the secondary element in your mind. Does the post still make sense?
  2. What reply is the poster expecting: answers and advice, or acknowledgement?
  3. Where is most of the content?

Code Seeking when the core purpose is to obtain information, advice or comfort, the question is substantive (specific, expecting a real answer), the sharing serves as background to the question, and the post would feel incomplete without the question.

Code Providing when the core purpose is to share or help, any question is incidental (politeness or inviting engagement), and the post keeps its value without the trailing question.

Incidental questions do not make a post Seeking: "Let me know what you think", "Hope this helps, any questions?", "Thoughts?", "Anyone else?", "Does that make sense?". Substantive questions do: "What dosage worked for you?", "Should I see a specialist?", "Has anyone experienced X side effect?".

Incidental emotional openers do not make a post emotional: "Sorry to hear that" followed by advice, "That sounds tough" followed by suggestions. The post is still Providing (and will be Informational in Step 2). Extended empathy, meaningful encouragement or genuine validation that forms the main content is substantive emotional support.

The 80/20 rule. If about 80% or more of a post shares or informs and only a brief question closes it, the question is incidental: Providing. If the question drives the structure of the post, the sharing is background: Seeking.

Priority when still uncertain: Seeking > Providing > None. If there is any genuine seeking element, code Seeking.

Worked examples (from the prompts):

  • "I've been on Fostair for 3 months now. It's really helped my symptoms, especially at night. The key is to rinse your mouth after using it. Let me know if you have questions!" Providing: 90% is shared experience; the closer is incidental.
  • "I was diagnosed last month and my GP put me on Ventolin. It helps sometimes but not always. Has anyone else found Ventolin inconsistent? Should I ask about something stronger?" Seeking: the background builds to two substantive questions.
  • "Just wanted to update you all - the surgery went well! I'm home now recovering. Thanks for all the good wishes." None: update and thanks, nothing sought or offered.
  • "I'm really struggling lately. My asthma has been terrible and I don't know what to do anymore. The medications aren't helping. I feel so exhausted." Seeking: distress with an implicit need for support, even without a question.
  • "I know exactly how you feel - it's so frightening when you can't breathe. You're not alone in this... Hang in there." Providing (emotional support).

3. Step 2: type of support (Informational, Emotional, Both)

Only for posts coded Seeking or Providing.

3a. Seeking: what does this person need from the community?

Informational (SIS). Facts, knowledge, advice or practical guidance: medical information, what to do and when, others' experiences to learn from, explanations, recommendations or referrals. Test: would a factual answer or practical advice satisfy the need?

Emotional (SES). Comfort, reassurance, validation or understanding: fear, worry, frustration, exhaustion, feeling overwhelmed; "Am I going to be okay?"; "Am I alone in feeling this?"; distress shared without a specific question. Test: would empathy, reassurance or "you're not alone" satisfy the primary need?

Both (SIS + SES). Only when there is a specific, substantive question and genuine emotional distress, both are significant parts of the post, and the poster would benefit from a factual answer and from emotional support. Typical shape: distress plus a specific question.

Rules:

  • Emotional words do not make a post Emotional. "I'm worried about my peak flow dropping - should I increase my preventer?" is Informational: the worry is context, the question is the point.
  • Questions do not make a post Informational. "Does anyone else feel like their life revolves around asthma?" and "Am I overreacting to be this worried?" are Emotional: they ask for resonance or reassurance, not facts.
  • Background versus core. "I'm worried because X happened. What should I do about Y?" is Informational. "I've tried X, Y, Z and nothing works. I'm exhausted and don't know how much more I can take." is Emotional.
  • Not Both: "Worried about symptoms, any advice?" is Informational (minimal emotion); "I'm really struggling" with a vague "what should I do?" is Emotional (the question is not specific).

Deciding in order (the rules above arranged as steps; nothing new):

  1. Is there a specific question whose answer would be facts, advice, or others' experience of a named thing (a drug, symptom, device, test, service)? If yes, an informational component is present.
  2. Is distress, or a request for reassurance, a substantial part of the post rather than a passing "a bit worried"? If yes, an emotional component is present.
  3. "Has anyone else ..." questions are decided by their object, not their tone: a thing (drug, device, test, treatment, service, symptom, diagnosis) or a situation ("been in a similar situation", "gone through this") is an experience request and counts as informational; a feeling or state of mind ("feel exhausted", "cope", "scared", "alone", "overreacting") is a validation request and counts as emotional. The emotional component is then judged separately under 2.
  4. Both present: Both. Only informational: Informational. Only emotional: Emotional.
  5. A narrative with no question at all: with distress, Emotional; a calm update, go back to Step 1 and consider None.

Examples: "My peak flow has dropped from 450 to 350 over the past week. Is this concerning? Should I contact my GP or wait?" Informational. "I was diagnosed last month and I'm absolutely terrified... I don't know how to live with this." Emotional. "I'm really anxious about my upcoming appointment with the respiratory consultant. What questions should I be asking?... I'm scared I'll forget everything." Both.

3b. Providing: what is this person offering to the community?

Informational (PIS). Facts, knowledge, advice or practical guidance: tips and strategies, experience shared in order to inform ("this worked for me"), resources and links, answers, suggestions including those in question form ("Have you tried X?"). Test: does it help someone know or do something?

Emotional (PES). Comfort, empathy, encouragement or validation: "I know how hard this is", "You can get through this", "It's normal to feel scared", "You're not alone". Test: is the poster mainly addressing someone's emotional needs?

Both (PIS + PES). Only when there is substantive practical information and substantive emotional support, each a meaningful part of the post, not a polite opener or closer.

Rules:

  • Polite openers and closers are not emotional support: "Sorry to hear that", "Hope this helps", "That's tough", "Good luck!". A post that is advice framed by these phrases is Informational.
  • Experience sharing can be either. "When I had X, I tried Y and it worked" informs: Informational. "I went through the same thing - it's really hard but it does get better" comforts: Emotional. Ask whether the purpose is to inform or to comfort.
  • Suggestions in question form are Informational: "Have you tried X?", "Why don't you ask your GP about Y?", "Have you thought about Z?".
  • Emotion in the poster's own story (joy, horror, frustration about their own case) is not emotional support; emotional support is directed at other readers.

Deciding in order: 1. Is there practical content a reader could act on or learn from (a tip, a resource, an experience told to inform, a suggestion in question form)? If yes, informational. 2. Is there comfort, empathy or encouragement that forms real content, beyond an opener or closer? If yes, emotional. Both: Both.

Examples: "When my asthma was at its worst, I found that sleeping with the head of my bed elevated really helped. Also, keeping a peak flow diary helped me spot patterns." Informational. "I completely understand how overwhelming this feels... You're doing better than you think. We're all here for you." Emotional. "Oh I really feel for you - that sounds incredibly stressful... What helped me was having a written action plan... But also, be kind to yourself." Both.

4. Step 3: posts coded None (COM, Ambiguous, or go back)

Before confirming None, check the post against the four support categories once more. If any of these applies, go back to Step 1 and recode the post as Seeking or Providing:

  • a request for advice, information, clarification or others' experiences, including implicit ones ("wondering if...", "not sure whether...") and one-line questions ("Mepolizumab - thoughts?");
  • worry, fear, frustration, sadness or feeling overwhelmed, reassurance seeking, or symptoms reported together with distress ("Feel like I keep getting fobbed off by my GP");
  • shared information, resources, advice or experience meant to inform others ("FYI: new guidelines");
  • comfort, encouragement, empathy or validation directed at others.

If none applies, choose one of:

COM (companionship). Social connection is the main purpose and a reply such as "That's nice!" or "Thanks for sharing!" would be appropriate: greetings and introductions without a request; community updates that seek no input ("Good news - the results were negative"); thanks without a new question; humour and casual conversation. A narrative of symptoms or treatment with no question is COM when it is calm or hopeful and Seeking, Emotional when it expresses worry, frustration or unease. Information mentioned in passing inside a thanks post or an introduction is not Providing, and stating one's own feelings inside an introduction ("glad to find this site") is not Seeking.

Ambiguous. The post cannot be classified: fragments without context ("Sharon 49", "Twice a day for twenty years"), near-empty posts, spam, garbled text, bare links without context.

Examples: "Update on sister - ... Dad phoned 111 at 2am for her. Sent to A&E, given steroids." is Seeking, Emotional: a worried narrative, not a neutral update. "Good news - I am going to have surgery on my nasal polyps!... So roll on Friday." is COM. "Evening - Hi still here in hospital... Fingers crossed feeling better not perfect but better." is COM. "Sick again - I have a stinker of a cold... I have to consider pulling my son out of nursery." is Seeking, Emotional (frustration, a hard decision shared for support).

5. Recording

Column Values When
intent Seeking, Providing, None every post
type Informational, Emotional, Both intent is Seeking or Providing
none_check COM, Ambiguous intent is None, after the Step 3 check
secondary Seeking, Providing optional: the other intent is also substantively present (would pass the Step 1 tests on its own), but is not the primary purpose; leave blank otherwise
notes free text optional; record doubts and borderline rules

The secondary column does not change the label. It records how often the one-intent rule has to choose between two substantive intents in this corpus, and is reported separately.

Final labels follow from the columns: Seeking + Informational = SIS; Seeking + Emotional = SES; Seeking + Both = SIS + SES; Providing + Informational = PIS; Providing + Emotional = PES; Providing + Both = PIS + PES; None + COM = COM; None + Ambiguous = excluded.
